Compatibility Percentages Are Mostly Fiction. Here's What Actually Works.

⏱ 11 min read ♥ Real synastry, plainly explained Updated 15 Oct 2026
The short version: Most "are we compatible?" tools add up your two Sun signs and return a number. That's not astrology — that's a horoscope with extra steps. Real compatibility lives in the angles between your charts, and it's messier and more useful than a percentage.

You've probably seen it: two birthdays in, one number out. "You and Alex are 74% compatible!" It feels authoritative. It's almost entirely meaningless, and understanding why is the fastest way to actually learn something useful about your relationships.

This guide explains real synastry — the comparison of two birth charts — in plain English. What it measures, what it can't, which contacts actually matter, and why the honest answer is usually more interesting than a score.

Why Sign Compatibility Tables Don't Work

The popular version goes: "Leos and Aquarians don't get along." There are two fatal problems with it.

Problem 1: A sign is a 30-degree range, not a point

Your Sun at 1° Scorpio and someone's Moon at 29° Scorpio are, astrologically, almost unrelated — they're 28 degrees apart inside the same label. A sign-based system treats them as identical. It's like saying two people are the same height because they both fall in the "160–190cm" bracket.

Real compatibility requires degrees. The actual angular distance between two planets is what creates an aspect, and it is frequently nothing like the sign relationship suggests.

Problem 2: Your Sun sign is about 8% of the picture

For dating, the placements that matter are Venus (how you love), Mars (how you pursue), the Moon (what makes you feel safe), Mercury (how you communicate), and your 7th House ruler (who you're wired to partner with). Your Sun sign is your identity — important for long-term fit, largely irrelevant to whether Tuesday's date goes well.

Two people with "incompatible" Sun signs can have a Venus–Mars trine that makes the attraction immediate and specific. Sign tables will never see it.

Aspects, Orbs, and Why Numbers Matter

An aspect is a specific angular distance between two points. In synastry you're measuring the distance between your planets and theirs.

☌ Conjunction · 0°

Same place in the sky. Fusion — you experience that planet together, intensely. Great with Venus or Moon; charged with Mars or Saturn.

△ Trine · 120°

Same element. Easy, natural flow. Effortless rapport — the aspect that makes people say "it just works."

⬡ Sextile · 60°

Friendly and productive, but needs initiative. Opportunity that only pays off if you take it.

□ Square · 90°

Friction. Persistent irritation that forces growth. Uncomfortable and frequently the reason relationships actually mature.

☍ Opposition · 180°

Polarity. Magnetic pull plus fundamental tension. Complementary if conscious, exhausting if not.

Orbs: the tolerance window

Planets almost never sit at exactly 120° apart. An orb is the allowed slack. Trines are typically read within 5–7 degrees, conjunctions and oppositions within 6–8, sextiles tighter at around 3–4.

Two things about orbs matter for you:

  • Tighter is stronger. A conjunction at 0.4° orb is dramatically more potent than one at 5.9°. Good analysis reports the orb, not just the aspect name.
  • Synastry orbs should be tighter than natal. Within one person's chart, a 6° square is meaningful — it's their character. Between two people, a 6° square is background noise compared to the actual relationship. Sloppy orbs manufacture connections that don't exist.
Where most tools cheat: they measure sign distance and multiply by 30°. Two planets four signs apart "must" be a trine. They frequently aren't — the real angle can be anywhere from 91° to 149°, which is a square-to-trine smear covering both. Roughly half of the aspects produced that way are fabricated. Always ask whether degrees were used.

The Five Contacts That Actually Matter

You don't need to compare everything. These are the pairings that reliably describe a relationship:

🔥 Venus ↔ Mars

Chemistry. The classic attraction axis, read in both directions — your Venus to their Mars, and their Venus to your Mars. Asymmetry here is common and informative.

🛡 Moon ↔ Moon

Emotional safety. Whether your nervous systems can co-regulate. The strongest predictor of whether daily life together feels restful or draining.

💬 Mercury ↔ Mercury

Communication. Can you actually understand each other? Cheap to fix, exhausting to ignore.

⏳ Saturn ↔ personal planets

Durability. Unpopular and underrated. Saturn glue is what keeps people together in year three. Often reads as "boring" early on.

⌂ Sun ↔ their 7th House / Descendant

Fit to their partnership axis. Whether you are structurally the type of person their chart is oriented toward.

☀ Sun ↔ Moon

Core-and-comfort alignment. A classic marriage aspect: identity and emotional needs pointing the same way.

Notice what's missing: your Sun signs. That's the whole point. Compatibility is a property of many small angles, not two labels.

"No Contact" Is Not a Bad Result

Here's something honest tools need to say and dishonest ones never will: sometimes there simply isn't an aspect. Your Venus sits at 40°, theirs at 275°, and nothing in the framework connects them.

Bad synastry apps handle this by defaulting to a made-up "neutral" score — usually 50% or 65% — so a number always comes out. That's not analysis, it's theatre. It hides the actual finding.

The real finding is: you two don't have angular contact in that dimension. That's genuinely useful. It means whatever you have going on isn't coming from that particular mechanism — no automatic chemistry channel, but also no built-in friction. You're building it consciously rather than being carried by it.

When this happens, the honest move is to exclude that dimension from any overall figure and tell you what fraction of the framework actually scored. A result based on 45% of the framework, clearly labelled, is worth infinitely more than a confident number made from nothing.

Contact and Ease Are Different Things

This distinction explains more relationship confusion than anything else in astrology.

  • Contact = how much angular binding exists between your charts. How often you land on each other's planets at all.
  • Ease = of that contact, how much is harmonious versus charged.

Collapsing them into one number is why percentages mislead. Four genuinely different situations all score "70%":

High contact, high ease

The easy one. Immediate rapport, little friction. Can underdevelop precisely because nothing forces growth.

High contact, low ease

The intense one. Magnetic, dramatic, hard to leave. Requires real conscious effort — but the energy is genuinely there.

Low contact, high ease

The comfortable one. Pleasant, undramatic, easy to be around. Nothing pushes, nothing pulls.

Low contact, low ease

Largely neutral. You're not wired to each other strongly in any direction — which means your actual behaviour matters more than your charts do.

Two people in the second category often have far more "chemistry" than two in the first — and a single percentage cannot tell you that.

Composite Charts: The Relationship Itself

Synastry compares two charts. A composite builds a third one: each point is the midpoint between your longitudes. Composite Sun is halfway between your two Suns.

What it's for: describing the relationship as its own entity. Composite Moon tends to show the emotional tone of the partnership — what it feels like to be "us" — which often differs from how either person feels individually.

It's a genuinely useful second lens, and it's computationally trivial once you have exact degrees (a midpoint calculation). Two honest caveats: the method is conventional rather than astronomical, and a midpoint of two withheld placements can't be computed at all. Good implementations omit those points instead of guessing them.

What To Do With This

Reading about synastry is fine, but it becomes useful the moment you apply it to a real pairing. The practical version:

  1. Get both charts computed properly — real planetary positions from dates, times and places, not sign tables.
  2. Look for contact before quality. Are there aspects at all? On which axes?
  3. Check the tightest orbs first. Anything under 2° will be obvious in daily life. Anything over 5° is background.
  4. Separate chemistry from safety from durability. They're independent, and mismatched expectations across them cause most of the confusion.
  5. Notice what's absent as carefully as what's present.

Where Compatibility Astrology Stops

Seriously — the limits matter as much as the method.

  • Charts describe tendencies, not people. Two people with near-identical compatibility readings can have completely different relationships, because they're different humans making different choices.
  • Hard aspects are not disqualifying. Squares and oppositions create the friction that makes relationships develop. Plenty of enduring partnerships are built on them.
  • Easy aspects are not a guarantee. Trines feel good and can also let two people coast past problems neither addresses.
  • No chart tells you about effort. The variable that decides most relationships isn't in either birth chart.
How to read all of this: Astrology is a reflective language — a structured way to notice patterns you'd otherwise miss. It is not a prediction engine, and no aspect entitles anyone to a verdict about another person. Use the map, then go talk to the human. They're the only actual evidence.
